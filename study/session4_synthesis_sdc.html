Physical Design Mentorship — Session 4

Logic Synthesis & SDC Fundamentals

MOSFET/CMOS → Standard Cells → Timing Fundamentals → Logic Synthesis & SDC → (next: Floorplanning)

Why This Session Matters

Physical design never starts from RTL. It starts from a gate-level netlist plus constraints. If either is poor, no amount of clever placement or CTS will rescue you. As a PD engineer you must be able to read a synthesis report, judge whether a netlist is a good starting point, and, most importantly, read and write SDC, because wrong constraints cause more wasted weeks than wrong tools.

TheoryDebugInterview Prep

1. Theory — What Synthesis Actually Does

RTL (Verilog)→ Translation (generic gates)→ Logic optimization→ Technology mapping (.lib cells)→ Gate-level netlist + reports

1.1 The Three Stages

  • Translation / elaboration: RTL is converted to a technology-independent netlist of generic logic (AND, OR, MUX, flops, adders). Your always blocks become flops and combinational clouds.
  • Logic optimization: Boolean restructuring, constant propagation, sharing of common sub-expressions, removing redundant logic. This is technology-independent.
  • Technology mapping: Generic logic is mapped onto real cells from the target library (the .lib you studied in Session 2), choosing cell types and drive strengths to meet the constraints.

1.2 Constraint-Driven Optimization

Synthesis is a three-way trade: timing, area, power. The tool does not know what you want, only what your constraints say. Typical priority: fix design-rule violations (max transition, max capacitance, max fanout) first, then timing (WNS/TNS), then area and power.

∑ Governing Equation · Timing Slack & TNS
$$\text{Slack} = T_{\text{required}} - T_{\text{arrival}} \quad \implies \quad \text{TNS} = \sum_{i \in \text{endpoints}} \min(0, \text{Slack}_i)$$
WNS (Worst Negative Slack): $\min(\text{Slack}_i)$ across all failing endpoints. TNS: cumulative sum of negative slack exposing problem breadth.
MetricMeaningWhat you check in the report
WNSWorst Negative Slack: the single worst failing pathShould be ≥ 0 at hand-off
TNSTotal Negative Slack: sum of all failing endpoints' slackTells you how widespread the problem is
Violating pathsNumber of failing endpointsFew paths = local fix; many = structural issue
AreaSum of cell areasSets floorplan size via utilization
DRVMax transition / cap / fanout violationsMust be clean before PD

1.3 Wire Load Models vs Physical-Aware Synthesis

Before placement, synthesis does not know real wire lengths. Older flows used wire load models (WLM): statistical wire estimates based on fanout. They are often wrong, which is why synthesis timing and post-route timing disagree. Modern flows use physical-aware / topographical synthesis (floorplan or placement information fed into synthesis), which gives much better correlation with the PD tool.

2. Theory — SDC (Synopsys Design Constraints)

SDC is a Tcl-based format that tells every tool in the flow (synthesis, PD, STA) what "correct timing" means. The same SDC, with updates, travels from synthesis to sign-off.

2.1 Clock Definition

create_clock -name clk -period 2.0 -waveform {0 1.0} [get_ports clk]
set_clock_uncertainty -setup 0.15 [get_clocks clk]
set_clock_uncertainty -hold  0.05 [get_clocks clk]
set_clock_latency 0.3 [get_clocks clk]          ;# pre-CTS only
set_clock_transition 0.1 [get_clocks clk]       ;# pre-CTS only

Latency and transition are ideal-clock assumptions used before CTS. After CTS the clock is propagated (set_propagated_clock) and these ideal values are removed.

2.2 I/O Constraints

set_input_delay  -clock clk -max 0.8 [get_ports data_in*]
set_input_delay  -clock clk -min 0.2 [get_ports data_in*]
set_output_delay -clock clk -max 0.6 [get_ports data_out*]
set_driving_cell -lib_cell BUFX2 [get_ports data_in*]
set_load 0.05 [get_ports data_out*]
∑ Physical Budgeting · I/O Path Timing Margin
$$T_{\text{internal\_budget}} = T_{\text{period}} - T_{\text{input\_delay}} - T_{\text{setup}} - T_{\text{uncertainty}}$$
Mentor's Perspective: set_input_delay models delay already burned external to your block. Logic inside has only $(T_{\text{period}} - T_{\text{input\_delay}})$ left.

2.3 Timing Exceptions

set_false_path -from [get_clocks clkA] -to [get_clocks clkB]
set_multicycle_path 2 -setup -from [get_pins regA/Q] -to [get_pins regB/D]
set_multicycle_path 1 -hold  -from [get_pins regA/Q] -to [get_pins regB/D]
set_clock_groups -asynchronous -group {clkA} -group {clkB}
ExceptionUse whenDanger
False pathPath exists structurally but can never be functionally exercised (or is async / static config)Hiding a real path = silicon failure
Multicycle pathData is intentionally given N cycles to propagateForgetting the matching hold adjustment
Clock groupsClocks are asynchronous or mutually exclusiveMasking real CDC paths that need synchronizers
∑ Rule of Thumb · Multicycle Hold Relationship
$$\text{Hold Check Cycle Shift} = N_{\text{setup}} - 1$$
The Rule: When setup is relaxed to $N$ cycles, STA automatically moves the default hold check $N-1$ cycles earlier. Restore it with set_multicycle_path (N-1) -hold.

2.4 Design Rule Constraints

set_max_transition 0.4 [current_design]
set_max_fanout 20 [current_design]
set_max_capacitance 0.2 [all_outputs]

3. What a PD Engineer Checks Before Accepting a Netlist

  1. No unconstrained paths. Run check_timing: look for unconstrained endpoints, missing clocks, no input delay.
  2. No unresolved references / black boxes (missing macros or cells).
  3. No assign statements or multiply-driven nets (unless intentional); netlist should be clean for PD import.
  4. WNS/TNS and DRV acceptable at the synthesis stage, with realistic margin because PD will add wire delay.
  5. Area in line with expectation (it drives the floorplan).
  6. Scan / DFT insertion done if required, and scan chains stitched, since this changes the netlist PD receives.
  7. Hierarchy decisions understood: flattened vs hierarchical (affects how you partition and budget later).

4. Debug Scenarios

🔴 Scenario A: Synthesis reports WNS = 0 and everything looks perfect, but PD shows hundreds of violations

The synthesis log is clean, yet the first placement run shows thousands of ps of negative slack. What do you suspect?

Walkthrough:
  1. Wire delay was never seen: Synthesis used an over-optimistic wire load model (WLM) or zero-interconnect delay. Check how interconnect was modeled.
  2. Constraints mismatch: Diff the SDC used in synthesis vs PD. A relaxed clock period or extra exceptions in the synthesis SDC would explain "too good" results.
  3. Over-optimistic exceptions: A broad set_false_path or set_clock_groups may have masked real paths from synthesis.
  4. Different library corner: Mismatch between synthesis and PD target libraries.
  5. Resolution: Re-run synthesis with physical-aware mode, align SDC/corners, and add realistic uncertainty margin.

🔴 Scenario B: After adding a multicycle path, a flood of hold violations appears

You set set_multicycle_path 2 -setup between two registers. Setup is now met easily, but hold reports show huge violations on that same path.

Walkthrough: You relaxed setup to 2 cycles but left hold at its default. With a 2-cycle setup, the default hold edge shifts one cycle earlier, so STA demands data stay stable for an extra full cycle. Fix: add set_multicycle_path 1 -hold on the same path, restoring hold to the original edge relationship.

🔴 Scenario C: check_timing reports "unconstrained endpoints"

Hundreds of flop D-pins show no timing check at all. Is that good news?

Walkthrough: No, it is dangerous: unconstrained means nobody is checking those paths, so they will never be optimized. Typical causes: the clock was defined on the wrong port/pin, a generated clock (divider output) was not declared, or a set_case_analysis / disable-timing is blocking the clock. Trace backwards to find where the clock definition stops.

5. Interview Questions

Q1: What are the inputs and outputs of logic synthesis?
A: Inputs: RTL, technology library (.lib, plus LEF/physical info if physical-aware), and constraints (SDC). Outputs: gate-level netlist, updated SDC, and reports (timing, area, power, DRV, constraint checks).
Q2: What does set_input_delay mean physically?
A: It models the delay consumed outside the chip (launch flop clock-to-Q plus board/previous-block logic) relative to a reference clock. The internal path from the input port to the first flop therefore has only (period − input delay − setup) available.
Q3: Difference between a false path and a multicycle path?
A: A false path is excluded from timing analysis entirely because it never functionally matters. A multicycle path is still analyzed, but with a relaxed capture edge because the design guarantees data has multiple cycles to propagate.
Q4: Why must you adjust hold when you set a multicycle setup of N?
A: STA picks the hold check relative to the setup capture edge. Moving setup to N cycles drags the hold check N−1 cycles away from where it should be. Applying -hold (N−1) pulls it back to the true launch-edge relationship.
Q5: Why do synthesis timing numbers differ from post-route numbers?
A: Synthesis estimates wires (WLM or rough placement), uses ideal clocks, and has no crosstalk, OCV, or real RC extraction. Post-route has propagated clocks, extracted parasitics, SI effects, and derates. Physical-aware synthesis narrows but never fully closes the gap.
Q6: What is the difference between ideal and propagated clocks?
A: An ideal clock has zero (or user-set) latency and transition at all sinks, which is used pre-CTS. A propagated clock uses the actual delays through the built clock tree, which is used post-CTS and in sign-off.
Q7: What is a generated clock and why must it be declared?
A: A clock derived from another (e.g., a divide-by-2 flop output). If not declared with create_generated_clock, STA does not know it is a clock, so the flops it drives become unconstrained.
Q8: Is flattening or keeping hierarchy better for PD?
A: Flat gives the tool more optimization freedom and usually better QoR, but is heavy on runtime/memory for large designs. Hierarchical allows parallel work and ECO isolation but needs careful timing budgeting at block boundaries. The choice is driven by design size and team structure.

6. Practice Task (Hands-on, 45–60 min)

  1. Take the counter/FSM RTL you wrote in Week 1 and write a complete SDC: clock, uncertainty, input/output delays, driving cell, load.
  2. Synthesize with Yosys + OpenSTA (or your OpenLane run) at two clock periods, one relaxed and one aggressive.
  3. Compare area and WNS between the two runs and write 3 sentences on what changed in the netlist (cell sizes, logic depth).
  4. Deliberately remove the create_clock line and run check_timing. Observe and note what the tool reports.
